3. Three differentiators nobody claims

The museum's hidden figures default, build first

from the asset lane and the founder lens · §6c kind: data

About 50 real sculptures baked to depth maps at build time: Poly Haven's bronze ray, whale and shark statues, marble bust, gothic statue, horse head (CC0); Smithsonian casts (CC0 per item); Kenney animals (CC0); Met 3D scans where CC0. About 40 backgrounds from the Met Open Access API: katagami stencils, William Morris, Iznik, The Grammar of Ornament; fabric and bark from ambientCG; Truchet and wallpaper-group tilings written in-house in the product's palette. Every picture prints its provenance line.

Cost: one evening of agent time, a Node bake script, no runtime 3D library (three.js would be 155 kB gzip; we ship ~3 MB of depth PNGs and ~6 MB of lazy-loaded tiles instead). Risk: licence hygiene; CI asserts each asset has a credit.

A book of plates default, with the library

from the London Stereoscopic Company 1862 and the Magic Eye books · the "battery of pre-generated stereograms"

~100 plates rendered at deploy time from the library, paged one per screen at #/book: a caption with the figure and the pattern, "find the angle" in Holbein's sense, a one-tap answer, and "make your own from this plate". Share-a-guess links carry parameters only, never the image (the geocachers' use). The first screen holds 12 plates; the rest lazy-load.

Cost: same pipeline as the library plus one screen and one journey. Metric: plate_open, plate_answer, conversion via book.

The patient eye next sprint

from NHS orthoptics, Wheatstone 1838 "requires practice", Thimbleby 1993 shimmer, Montessori · §6c kind: method

A six-step course on the See page, staged like the orthoptists' cat → bucket → ABC/123: shallow wide sphere, a block, letters, the default, a plate from the book, a drawn one, with a relax step between and the viewer marking "seen it" themself. A screen-only shimmer toggle from the algorithm's own authors. The last rung states the honest figure: about 7 % of adults under 60 are stereoblind (Chopin et al. 2019), so our guide's "one in twenty" is corrected to "about one in fourteen".

Cost: ~150 lines, one journey. Competitors offer Easy/Normal/Hard at most.

4. Beautiful backgrounds: what "beautiful" means here

One caveat from the asset lane, untested on real pictures: strongly periodic horizontal ornament creates false matches. The bake step measures horizontal autocorrelation and keeps mid-frequency, non-repeating tiles; the renderer already refits each tile to the strip width.

5. Other ideas from the fringe, parked with their source

  • Landscapes from public-domain elevation tiles: Grand Canyon, Etna, the Tatras, Tycho crater. Nobody does this. (Terrarium tiles, USGS)
  • Every export in three versions: parallel, cross-eyed, depth map, as Young Rival did for their video, because people manage only one method.
  • Text-only .txt stereogram for geocachers and puzzle makers (CacheSleuth, the Czech "Magické oko" cache).
  • A decoder beside the generator, and the demo "a picture your AI cannot read": frontier models misread hidden shapes in Edwardsson's 2026 test.
  • Dual-scene and animated stereograms, with the 1996 TU Wien taxonomy and the Hackaday rule (re-randomise the pattern per frame or tiles slide).
  • Physical export: woven, knitted or laser-cut from the same depth map (Breedon; UW illusion knitting).
  • The Dalí halo: Julesz's random-dot torus used as a saint's halo, told in the book as the first religious-art hidden figure.

6. Refused on purpose

  • On-device AI depth estimation as the headline (Owl3D, Tembrica): a 28–50 MB model and the same product as everyone else. Users bring depth maps; the page stays deterministic.
  • "Magic Eye" in the product name: a live trademark. Descriptive use in copy only.
  • Watermarks, upsells, accounts, badges, eye-exercise claims beyond the 2013 Latvia thesis.
